Failure-to-deliver · SEC bi-monthly data, charted by ChartExchange GME FTD ↗
GME failures to deliver

Shares sold but not delivered by the settlement date, from the SEC's fails-to-deliver file (published twice a month, roughly two weeks in arrears). A high or persistent FTD count is often read as a sign of naked shorting or settlement stress, though routine operational fails land here too.

ChartExchange sends X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN so it cannot be embedded, and no free CORS API carries this series, so the chart and table open there in a new tab: chartexchange.com ↗

Institutional holdings · who owns GME and how positions moved last quarter Fintel ↗
GME institutional ownership

Shares held by funds, banks and advisers, from quarterly 13F filings plus any 13D/13G stakes. Roughly 45% of the share count sits with institutions — Vanguard, BlackRock and State Street are the three largest — and the quarter-on-quarter change per holder is what most people read this page for. Filings lag the market by up to 45 days.

Fintel gates its pages behind a Cloudflare bot check and a paywall, and no free CORS API carries this data, so it cannot be embedded here. Open it directly, or use Nasdaq's ownership page, which is free: fintel.io ↗  ·  nasdaq.com ↗

— Bi-monthly SI ↗

FINRA's daily short volume for GME — shares sold short that session as a share of FINRA-reported volume (cdn.finra.org, CORS-open, no key, posted after each close). This is a daily flow figure: it counts market-maker hedging, does not net out same-day buy-to-cover, and excludes on-exchange lit volume, so it runs well above true short interest and is best read as a trend rather than a level.

True short interest (bi-monthly)

The headline “% of float sold short” and days-to-cover come from FINRA's settlement-date report twice a month. No free CORS feed carries that series — Nasdaq's API omits Access-Control-Allow-Origin, and ChartExchange and Fintel are bot-gated — so it opens in a new tab: nasdaq.com ↗  ·  chartexchange.com ↗
